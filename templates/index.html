Legal Hash Checksum — Complete Guide

Everything about digital evidence hashing under BSA 2023 Section 63(4)(c)

What is a Legal Hash Checksum?

A legal hash checksum is a unique alphanumeric fingerprint of a digital file. Under BSA 2023 Section 63(4)(c), Indian courts require a hash value to certify that digital evidence has not been tampered with since seizure.

What is a Hash Checksum Tool?

A checksum tool or hash generator computes the hash value of any file. This free online hash generator computes MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256 and SHA-512 entirely in your browser — the file never leaves your device.

MD5 Hash vs SHA-256 — Which is accepted in Indian Courts?

Both MD5 hash and SHA-256 hash are accepted under BSA 2023. Courts increasingly prefer SHA-256 as it is more secure. Best practice is to include all four algorithms in your hash report.

How to fill Part A and Part B Certificate?

Part A is filled by the party producing evidence — the device owner. Part B is filled by a forensic expert. This tool auto-fills hash values into both certificates. Just add your details and sign before submitting to court.

What is Section 63(4)(c) of BSA 2023?

Section 63(4)(c) of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 mandates that electronic records submitted as evidence must carry a certificate stating the hash value, signed by the producing party or a forensic expert.

Is this tool safe? Does it upload files?

No files are uploaded. This file checksum tool runs 100% in your browser using the Web Crypto API. This preserves chain of custody — critical for police digital evidence, forensic experts, and court submissions.

Who needs a Legal Hash Certificate in India?

  • Police officers — cyber crime evidence, seized devices, FIR attachments
  • Advocates — filing digital evidence on behalf of clients in court
  • Forensic experts — certifying evidence integrity with Part B certificate
  • Corporate legal teams — email, document, and server data as evidence
  • Cyber crime investigators — hashing digital artifacts and storage media
  • IT departments — producing computer records and logs as court evidence
